1. PROJECT OVERVIEW
The AgriChain Smart System is the flagship project of the Innovation Lab Learning Ecosystem. The project serves as both a learning and development platform and a technology development initiative for Interns. Through this project, participants develop research capabilities, technical competencies, innovation skills, and practical experience while contributing to the design and development of a real-world intelligent agricultural solution.
The project adopts a project-based learning approach where research, learning, innovation, and technology development occur simultaneously. Participants are expected to learn through investigation, experimentation, implementation, collaboration, and continuous improvement while contributing to the AgriChain Smart System.
The project integrates contributions from all Innovation Lab technical streams:

PHASE 1: RESEARCH, DISCOVERY AND PLANNING
Duration: Week 1
1.1 Phase Overview
The Research, Discovery and Planning Phase serves as the foundation of the AgriChain Smart System flagship project. During this phase, participants investigate the agricultural problem domain, existing agricultural solutions, relevant technologies, applicable methods and tools, available datasets, and opportunities for improvement.
The purpose of this phase is to establish a research-informed foundation for the project and to ensure that all future technical, innovation, and development activities are guided by evidence and an understanding of the problem being addressed.
By the end of this phase, participants should understand the agricultural challenges relevant to AgriChain, the technologies available within their stream, the strengths and limitations of existing solutions, and how their stream can contribute to the successful development of the AgriChain Smart System.
1.2 Phase Objectives
Develop a common understanding of the AgriChain Smart System vision and purpose.
Understand the agricultural challenges being addressed.
Investigate existing agricultural technology solutions.
Identify technologies relevant to each technical stream.
Investigate applicable methods, algorithms, frameworks, tools, sensors, and architectures.
Identify datasets suitable for experimentation, simulation, and testing.
Identify limitations within existing solutions.
Identify opportunities for improvement and innovation.
Define the contribution of each stream to AgriChain.
Establish a research-informed foundation for future project activities.

2.6 Detailed Stream Milestone Guide for Phase 2 and Phase 3 Development
The following milestones strengthen the roadmap by giving each stream measurable development checkpoints. They do not replace the phase structure; they sit inside the phases as implementation targets and evidence of progress.
Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning Stream
Milestone 1: Build a System That Can Identify Crop Types From a Photo
Build a simple AI tool that can look at a photo of a crop and identify the type of crop.
Use a ready-made AI model such as MobileNet or ResNet and train it to recognise at least 5 different crops commonly grown in South Africa.
Test the model using a sufficient dataset of crop photos to confirm it gives the correct answer at least 80% of the time.
Build a simple app screen where a farmer can take or upload a photo and instantly see the crop name as the result.
Document every step of the process so the work can be reviewed and built upon in Milestone 2.
Milestone 2: Teach the System to Spot Diseases in Crops
Download and prepare a free crop disease image dataset such as PlantVillage containing photos of both healthy and diseased crops for training.
Train the model to identify at least 10 different crop diseases, pest damage signs, or nutrient problems.
Test the model using at least 300 photos and confirm it gives the correct diagnosis at least 75% of the time.
Display the diagnosis result together with a confidence percentage so the farmer knows how certain the system is.
Present the result in plain, simple language that a farmer with no technical background can understand.
Milestone 3: Add a Treatment Recommendation Feature
Use the disease diagnosis result from Milestone 2 as the starting point for generating treatment advice.
For each diagnosis, display at least 3 treatment options ranked from most to least recommended.
Each treatment option must include whether it is organic or chemical, how much to use, and how to prevent the disease in future.
Test the recommendations using at least 50 different diagnosis scenarios and have an agriculture expert review and approve the results.
Share the system with at least 10 small-scale farmers, collect their feedback using a simple survey, and achieve a satisfaction score of at least 80%.
Internet of Things (IoT) Stream
Milestone 1: Build a System That Collects and Displays Farm Sensor Data
Set up at least 3 IoT sensors such as a Raspberry Pi or Arduino kit to measure soil moisture, soil temperature, and nutrient levels in a test field or simulated environment.
Connect the sensors to a central system that collects and stores readings automatically every 30 minutes.
Build a simple dashboard that displays the live sensor readings in a clear, easy-to-read format for the farmer.
Test the system over a period of at least 2 weeks to confirm that data is being captured consistently and accurately with no more than 10% data loss.
Document the hardware setup and software configuration so the work can be reviewed and built upon in Milestone 2.
Milestone 2: Track Weather Changes and Automate Irrigation Schedules
Integrate a weather data source such as a local weather API or an additional weather sensor to track real-time temperature, humidity, and rainfall in the farm area.
Use the combined soil and weather data to generate an automated daily irrigation schedule that adjusts based on current conditions.
Send the farmer a notification via SMS or app alert at least once a day with the recommended irrigation schedule and the reason for the recommendation.
Test the automated scheduling system over at least 3 weeks and confirm that irrigation recommendations respond correctly to at least 5 different simulated weather and soil conditions.
Measure water usage before and after automation is applied and demonstrate a reduction in scheduled water output of at least 15%.
Milestone 3: Add Automated Pest Detection Alerts
Research and identify at least 5 environmental conditions such as humidity thresholds, temperature ranges, or soil changes that are known to indicate pest activity for the selected crop types.
Programme the sensor system to continuously monitor for these conditions and trigger an alert when two or more indicators are detected at the same time.
Deliver the alert to the farmer within 15 minutes of detection via SMS, email, or app notification, clearly stating the type of threat and the affected area.
Test the alert system using at least 20 simulated pest condition scenarios and confirm that the correct alert is triggered in at least 85% of cases.
Share the system with at least 10 small-scale farmers, collect feedback through a simple survey, and achieve a satisfaction score of at least 80%.
Drone Technology Stream
Milestone 1: Build a System that Captures and Displays Aerial Field Maps Using a Drone
Use an available drone or drone simulator to fly a planned grid route over a test field of at least 1 hectare and capture a minimum of 50 overlapping aerial photographs.
Use free photogrammetry software such as OpenDroneMap or WebODM to stitch the photographs into a single, georeferenced field map.
Confirm the output map covers 100% of the planned flight area with a ground resolution of at least 5 cm per pixel.
Display the completed field map on a simple dashboard where the farmer can zoom in and inspect different areas of the land.
Document the flight plan, software settings, and map output so the process can be repeated and built upon in Milestone 2.
Milestone 2: Use Drone Imagery to Identify Crop Health Zones and Generate Targeted Application Maps
Process the aerial imagery from Milestone 1 using a vegetation index such as NDVI or a visible-light equivalent to classify field zones into at least 3 health categories: healthy, stressed, and critical.
Generate a colour-coded crop health map that clearly marks which zones require treatment, displayed on the farmer dashboard.
Produce a targeted application map that specifies which areas of the field require pesticide or fertiliser treatment, reducing the application area by at least 20% compared to a blanket-spray approach.
Test the zone classification against at least 30 field sample points and confirm the health categorisation matches ground-truth observations in at least 75% of cases.
Document the analysis method and outputs clearly so an intern with no prior GIS experience can reproduce the process.
Milestone 3: Feed Drone Imagery into the AI Crop Disease Detection Model to Improve Accuracy
Extract and label a minimum of 200 crop image patches from the drone field maps captured in Milestones 1 and 2, tagging each patch as healthy, diseased, or stressed.
Add the labelled drone image patches to the existing AI training dataset and retrain the disease detection model using the combined dataset.
Compare the retrained model detection accuracy against the original AI model using the same test dataset and demonstrate an improvement of at least 5% in detection accuracy.
Build a simple automated pipeline that accepts a new drone field map as input and outputs a disease risk overlay map highlighting areas of concern across the entire field.
Pilot the end-to-end system with at least 5 small-scale farmers, collect structured feedback using a simple survey, and achieve a satisfaction score of at least 80%.
Cybersecurity Stream
Milestone 1: Protect Farmer Data and Financial Transactions Using End-to-End Encryption
Identify all data fields in the platform that contain sensitive information such as farmer personal details, bank details, transaction amounts, and harvest records, and produce a written data classification list before any encryption is applied.
Implement AES-256 encryption for all sensitive data stored in the platform database, confirming that no sensitive field is saved in plain text.
Enforce HTTPS across all platform pages and API endpoints using a valid SSL/TLS certificate, ensuring all data sent between the farmer device and the server is encrypted in transit.
Test the encryption by attempting to read stored sensitive data directly from the database and confirming that the output is unreadable without the correct decryption key in at least 10 test cases.
Document the encryption approach, key management process, and test results so the implementation can be reviewed and verified by a supervisor.
Milestone 2: Build a Role-Based Access Control System Across All Platform Modules
Define at least 4 distinct user roles for the platform such as farmer, buyer, administrator, and guest, and document exactly which platform features and data each role is allowed to access.
Implement role-based access control so that each user account is assigned a role at registration and the system automatically restricts access to features and data outside that role.
Test the access control system using at least 20 test cases covering both authorised and unauthorised access attempts and confirm that unauthorised access is blocked in 100% of test cases.
Ensure that all failed access attempts are automatically logged with the user account, timestamp, and action attempted, and that these logs are visible to the administrator role only.
Conduct a peer review where one intern attempts to access restricted areas of the platform using a lower-permission account and documents any gaps found, with all gaps resolved before sign-off.
Milestone 3: Secure IoT Device Communications Against External Threats
Implement mutual TLS or an equivalent secure communication protocol between the IoT sensors and the central platform server, ensuring all sensor data is encrypted in transit.
Assign each IoT device a unique device identity such as a certificate or API key so the platform can verify that incoming data is coming from a known, trusted device and not a fake one.
Set up an automated alert that notifies the platform administrator within 5 minutes if an unrecognised device attempts to connect to the sensor network or if a known device sends data outside its normal range.
Simulate at least 5 common IoT attack scenarios such as a man-in-the-middle attack, a spoofed sensor, and an unauthorised connection attempt and confirm the system detects and blocks each one.
Pilot the secured IoT communication system with at least 10 small-scale farmers, collect feedback using a simple survey, and achieve a satisfaction score of at least 80% on ease of use and perceived security.
Cloud Computing Stream
Milestone 1: Deploy the Platform on a Scalable Cloud Environment with Reliable Uptime
Select and set up a free or low-cost cloud hosting environment such as AWS Free Tier, Google Cloud Free Tier, or Microsoft Azure for Students and deploy a working version of the platform to it.
Configure the cloud server with automatic restart capabilities so that if the server crashes or restarts, the platform comes back online within 2 minutes without manual action.
Set up a basic uptime monitoring tool such as UptimeRobot that checks whether the platform is online every 5 minutes and sends an alert to the team if it goes down.
Run a 2-week uptime test and confirm the platform achieves an availability rate of at least 95%.
Document the full deployment process step by step so that any intern can redeploy the platform on a new server by following the written instructions alone.
Milestone 2: Optimise the Platform for Low-Bandwidth Conditions in Rural Areas
Measure the current data usage of each main platform page or feature by recording the total page size in kilobytes before any optimisation is applied, creating a baseline to compare against.
Apply at least 3 optimisation techniques such as image compression, lazy loading of images, minification of CSS and JavaScript files, caching of repeated content, and reduction of unnecessary API calls.
After optimisation, confirm that the total data loaded on the main farmer-facing page is no more than 500 KB so that it loads comfortably on a 3G mobile connection.
Test the platform load time by simulating a slow 3G connection using browser developer tools and confirm the main page loads fully within 5 seconds.
Add an offline mode or basic caching feature using a service worker or equivalent that allows the farmer to view the last known dashboard data when internet connectivity is temporarily lost.
Milestone 3: Make the Platform Scalable so it Can Grow as More Farmers Join
Set up auto-scaling on the cloud environment so that additional server capacity is automatically added when the number of active users exceeds a defined threshold, for example when CPU usage goes above 70% for more than 2 minutes.
Use a load testing tool such as Apache JMeter or Locust to simulate at least 200 simultaneous users accessing the platform and confirm the system maintains an average response time of under 3 seconds throughout the test.
Set up a content delivery network to serve static platform files from a server location closest to the user, and confirm this reduces average load time by at least 20% compared to without a CDN.
Configure automated daily database backups to a separate cloud storage location and test the restore process by successfully recovering the platform from a backup within 30 minutes.
Pilot the fully deployed, optimised and scalable platform with at least 10 small-scale farmers across at least 2 different rural locations in South Africa, collect feedback using a simple survey, and achieve a satisfaction score of at least 80%.
Blockchain and Traceability Extension Stream
Milestone 1: Build a Crop Inventory and Harvest Record System on a Blockchain
Set up a test blockchain environment using a free beginner-friendly platform such as Ethereum Testnet, Hyperledger Fabric, or Ganache that interns can run locally without real money.
Build a simple data entry form where a farmer can log a harvest record including crop type, quantity harvested, harvest date, and farm location.
Store each harvest record as a transaction on the blockchain so that once it is saved it cannot be edited or deleted.
Display a clear history of all logged harvest records on a farmer dashboard, showing at least the last 10 entries with their blockchain transaction reference.
Test the system by logging at least 20 harvest records and confirming that all entries are stored correctly and remain unchanged on the blockchain ledger.
Document the setup and data structure so the work can be extended in Milestone 2.
Milestone 2: Track Produce from Farm to Market Using the Blockchain Ledger
Extend the Milestone 1 system to allow additional supply chain events to be recorded for each harvest batch, including produce packed, dispatched from farm, received by distributor, and delivered to market or buyer.
Assign each harvest batch a unique tracking ID such as a QR code that links all supply chain events together on the blockchain from farm to final destination.
Build a simple public-facing lookup page where a consumer can scan or enter the tracking ID and see the full journey of the produce displayed in a clear, easy-to-read timeline.
Test the full tracking flow using at least 10 simulated produce batches, each passing through a minimum of 4 supply chain stages, and confirm all events are recorded correctly and in the correct order.
Confirm that no previously recorded supply chain event can be altered or deleted once it has been added to the blockchain, demonstrating tamper-proof traceability.
Milestone 3: Automate Buyer-Seller Payments Using Smart Contracts
Write a basic smart contract using Solidity or an equivalent beginner-friendly language that holds a buyer payment in escrow and releases it to the farmer automatically when a confirmed delivery event is recorded on the blockchain.
Test the smart contract using at least 15 simulated transactions covering successful delivery and payment release, failed delivery and payment refund, and partial delivery with proportional payment.
Integrate the smart contract into the tracking system so that a confirmed delivered-to-buyer event automatically triggers payment release without manual action.
Display the payment status as pending, released, or refunded on both the farmer dashboard and the buyer-facing view, updating in real time as blockchain events are recorded.
Pilot the full system - inventory, traceability, and smart contract payment - with at least 10 small-scale farmers and achieve a user satisfaction score of at least 80% based on a simple structured survey.
